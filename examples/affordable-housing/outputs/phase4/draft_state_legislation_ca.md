## LEGISLATIVE COUNSEL'S DIGEST

AB XXXX, as introduced, ________.

**Land use: Housing Opportunity Standards.**

Existing law, the Planning and Zoning Law, requires a city or county to adopt a general plan for land use development, including a housing element. Existing law requires the housing element to contain an assessment of housing needs, an inventory of resources and constraints, and a program to implement the goals of the element.

This bill would require cities and counties to comply with Housing Opportunity Standards, as defined, to maintain eligibility for specified federal transportation funding. The bill would require the Department of Housing and Community Development to establish a certification process and provide technical assistance.

## THE PEOPLE OF THE STATE OF CALIFORNIA DO ENACT AS FOLLOWS:

### SECTION 1. Legislative Findings and Declarations.

The Legislature finds and declares all of the following:

(a) California faces a severe housing shortage, with a deficit of approximately 1,500,000 units needed to meet current demand.

(b) Housing costs in California are among the highest in the nation, with median home prices exceeding eight times median household income in many metropolitan areas.

(c) Local land use restrictions, including exclusionary single-family zoning, have contributed significantly to housing underproduction and unaffordability.

(d) The federal National Zoning Reform Act of 2026 conditions federal transportation funding on state and local adoption of Housing Opportunity Standards.

(e) California receives approximately $8,000,000,000 annually in federal transportation funding that would be at risk under the federal act if localities fail to comply.

(f) This act is necessary to ensure California's continued eligibility for federal transportation funding and to advance the state's housing production goals.

(g) While this act responds to federal requirements, California has an independent interest in promoting housing affordability and accessibility for all residents.

## CHAPTER 4.7. HOUSING OPPORTUNITY COMPLIANCE ACT

### Article 1. General Provisions

#### 65918.200. Short Title.

This chapter shall be known, and may be cited, as the "Housing Opportunity Compliance Act."

#### 65918.201. Purpose.

The purpose of this chapter is to:

(a) Ensure California's continued eligibility for federal transportation funding under the National Zoning Reform Act of 2026.

(b) Increase housing production, particularly near transit and employment centers.

(c) Reduce housing costs for California residents.

(d) Protect existing residents from displacement while promoting new housing development.

(e) Provide technical assistance to local governments in achieving compliance.

#### 65918.202. Definitions.

For purposes of this chapter, the following definitions apply:

(a) "Accessory dwelling unit" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 65852.2.

(b) "By-right" has the same meaning as set forth in Section 65583.2.

(c) "Department" means the Department of Housing and Community Development.

(d) "Eligible local agency" means a city or county that has land use authority over areas within one-half mile of a fixed transit station, as defined by federal law.

(e) "Federal act" means the National Zoning Reform Act of 2026, Public Law XXX-XXX.

(f) "Housing opportunity standards" means the standards established by the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development pursuant to the federal act.

(g) "Transit-oriented development zone" means the area within one-half mile of any fixed transit station, as defined by federal law.

---

### Article 2. Housing Opportunity Standards

#### 65918.210. Compliance Required.

(a) Each eligible local agency shall adopt and enforce local ordinances that meet or exceed the housing opportunity standards established pursuant to the federal act.

(b) Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to limit the authority of a local agency to adopt ordinances that exceed the housing opportunity standards.

#### 65918.211. Transit-Oriented Density.

(a) Within transit-oriented development zones, eligible local agencies shall permit multifamily residential development as a by-right use at densities not less than those specified in the federal act.

(b) Eligible local agencies shall not require conditional use permits, planned development permits, or other discretionary approvals for residential development in transit-oriented development zones that meets objective zoning and design standards.

(c) Eligible local agencies may adopt objective design standards for development in transit-oriented development zones, provided that those standards:

(1) Do not have the effect of precluding compliance with density requirements.

(2) Are adopted pursuant to Section 65913.4 or otherwise comply with state law regarding objective standards.

(3) Do not require public hearings for individual projects.

#### 65918.212. Accessory Dwelling Units.

In addition to the requirements of Section 65852.2, eligible local agencies shall ensure that their accessory dwelling unit ordinances comply with the housing opportunity standards, including:

(a) Permitting accessory dwelling units by right on any lot zoned for single-family residential use.

(b) Not requiring owner occupancy.

(c) Not requiring additional parking.

(d) Permitting units of at least 800 square feet or 50 percent of primary dwelling floor area.

#### 65918.213. Parking Reform.

Within transit-oriented development zones, eligible local agencies shall not impose minimum parking requirements for residential development.

#### 65918.214. Approval Timelines.

(a) For residential development within transit-oriented development zones that meets objective zoning and design standards, eligible local agencies shall:

(1) Determine application completeness within 30 days.

(2) Complete all reviews within 60 days of a complete application.

(3) Issue permits within 90 days of a complete application.

(b) If an eligible local agency fails to act within the timelines specified in subdivision (a), the application shall be deemed approved pursuant to the federal act.

---

### Article 3. Anti-Displacement Protections

#### 65918.220. Required Protections.

To be certified as compliant with this chapter, an eligible local agency shall adopt and enforce all of the following protections:

(a) Just-cause eviction protections consistent with the federal act.

(b) Tenant right of first refusal for properties with five or more rental units.

(c) Inclusionary housing requirements as specified in the federal act.

(d) Relocation assistance for tenants displaced by demolition or substantial rehabilitation.

#### 65918.221. Coordination with State Law.

(a) The protections required by Section 65918.220 shall be in addition to, and not in lieu of, any protections provided by state law, including Section 1946.2 of the Civil Code.

(b) In the event of a conflict between this chapter and state law, the provision providing greater protection to tenants shall apply.

#### 65918.222. Local Enhancement.

Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to limit the authority of a local agency to adopt tenant protections that exceed those required by this chapter or the federal act.

---

### Article 4. Certification and Compliance

#### 65918.230. Certification Process.

(a) Not later than January 1, 2030, each eligible local agency shall submit to the department a certification of compliance with this chapter.

(b) The department shall establish a certification process consistent with federal requirements.

(c) Certifications shall be submitted in a form prescribed by the department and shall include:

(1) Copies of all relevant ordinances.

(2) Documentation of permit processing timelines.

(3) Evidence of anti-displacement protections.

(4) Any other information required by the department.

#### 65918.231. Review and Approval.

(a) The department shall review certifications for completeness and compliance within 60 days of submission.

(b) The department shall notify local agencies of any deficiencies and provide an opportunity to cure.

(c) Upon determination of compliance, the department shall issue a certificate of compliance valid for one year.

#### 65918.232. Annual Recertification.

Each eligible local agency shall submit annual recertifications demonstrating continued compliance.

#### 65918.233. State Certification to Federal Government.

The department shall compile local certifications and submit a statewide certification to the federal Department of Housing and Urban Development consistent with federal requirements.

---

### Article 5. Technical Assistance

#### 65918.240. Technical Assistance Program.

(a) The department shall establish a technical assistance program to assist eligible local agencies in achieving compliance with this chapter.

(b) Technical assistance may include:

(1) Model ordinance development.

(2) Planning capacity grants.

(3) Training for local planning officials.

(4) Compliance monitoring tools.

#### 65918.241. Priority Assistance.

The department shall prioritize technical assistance to:

(a) Local agencies that have not previously adopted zoning reforms.

(b) Local agencies with limited planning capacity.

(c) Local agencies serving disadvantaged communities.

#### 65918.242. Appropriation.

For purposes of implementing the technical assistance program, there is hereby appropriated [Amount] from the General Fund to the department.

---

### Article 6. Enforcement

#### 65918.250. Consequences of Non-Compliance.

(a) An eligible local agency that fails to achieve certification by the deadline specified in Section 65918.230 shall be subject to the consequences specified in the federal act, including potential reduction in federal transportation funding.

(b) The department shall notify local agencies of impending federal deadlines not less than one year in advance.

#### 65918.251. State Remedies.

(a) If an eligible local agency fails to adopt compliant ordinances within the time required, the department may adopt model ordinances that shall apply to that local agency.

(b) Any person may bring an action in superior court to compel compliance with this chapter.

(c) The court may award reasonable attorney's fees and costs to a prevailing plaintiff.

#### 65918.252. Reports to the Legislature.

(a) On or before January 1, 2029, and annually thereafter, the department shall submit a report to the Legislature on:

(1) The compliance status of each eligible local agency.

(2) Housing production trends in transit-oriented development zones.

(3) The effectiveness of anti-displacement protections.

(4) Recommendations for program improvements.

(b) A report to the Legislature pursuant to this section shall be submitted in compliance with Section 9795 of the Government Code.

---

### Article 7. Miscellaneous

#### 65918.260. Relationship to Other Law.

(a) This chapter shall be interpreted to complement and not conflict with existing state housing law, including but not limited to:

(1) The Housing Accountability Act (Section 65589.5).

(2) The Density Bonus Law (Section 65915).

(3) Accessory dwelling unit law (Section 65852.2).

(4) The Housing Element Law (Article 10.6 (commencing with Section 65580)).

(b) To the extent any conflict exists between this chapter and other state law, the provision that results in greater housing production or greater tenant protection shall apply.

#### 65918.261. Severability.

If any provision of this chapter or its application is held invalid, that invalidity shall not affect other provisions or applications that can be given effect without the invalid provision or application.

#### 65918.262. Operative Date.

This chapter shall become operative on January 1, 2027.

---

### SECTION 3. No Reimbursement.

No reimbursement is required by this act pursuant to Section 6 of Article XIII B of the California Constitution because a local agency or school district has the authority to levy service charges, fees, or assessments sufficient to pay for the program or level of service mandated by this act, within the meaning of Section 17556 of the Government Code.

Alternatively, no reimbursement is required because this act implements a federal law that would otherwise result in loss of federal funding, within the meaning of Section 17556 of the Government Code.
