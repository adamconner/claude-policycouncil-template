## National Zoning Reform Act of 2026

**Policy Council Phase 4 | Legislative Counsel Draft**

---

## H.R. XXXX / S. XXXX

### A BILL

To condition certain Federal transportation funding on State and local adoption of land use policies that promote housing affordability and accessibility, and for other purposes.

---

**IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES / IN THE SENATE**

---

## SECTION 1. SHORT TITLE.

This Act may be cited as the "National Zoning Reform Act of 2026" or the "NZRA".

---

## SECTION 2. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE.

### (a) FINDINGS.—Congress finds the following:

(1) The United States faces a shortage of approximately 3,800,000 housing units, the largest housing deficit since World War II.

(2) Local land use restrictions, including exclusionary zoning, contribute significantly to housing costs, with research indicating that such restrictions add 20 to 50 percent to housing costs in high-demand metropolitan areas.

(3) Housing constraints in high-productivity cities have reduced aggregate United States economic growth by limiting worker mobility to areas of greater economic opportunity.

(4) Federal investments in transit infrastructure are underutilized when local zoning prohibits housing development near transit stations.

(5) The historical origins of single-family-only zoning include intentional racial and economic exclusion, and such zoning continues to perpetuate residential segregation.

(6) Multiple States have enacted zoning reform legislation, demonstrating that such reforms are administratively feasible and can be implemented without disrupting community character.

### (b) PURPOSE.—The purpose of this Act is to—

(1) increase the supply of housing in the United States, particularly near public transit and employment centers;

(2) reduce housing costs for American families;

(3) maximize the return on Federal investments in transit infrastructure;

(4) promote economic productivity and labor mobility;

(5) address the legacy of exclusionary zoning while protecting existing residents from displacement; and

(6) provide technical assistance to State and local governments in implementing zoning reforms.

---

## SECTION 3. DEFINITIONS.

In this Act:

### (1) ACCESSORY DWELLING UNIT.—
The term "accessory dwelling unit" means a smaller, secondary housing unit located on the same lot as a primary residential dwelling, including—
- (A) detached units;
- (B) attached units;
- (C) units within or converted from existing structures; and
- (D) units above garages.

### (2) BY-RIGHT APPROVAL.—
The term "by-right approval" means a land use approval process that—
- (A) is administrative and ministerial in nature;
- (B) does not require discretionary review, conditional use permits, or public hearings;
- (C) applies objective, predetermined standards; and
- (D) results in approval if the application meets such standards.

### (3) COVERED FUNDING.—
The term "covered funding" means funds made available under—
- (A) the Surface Transportation Block Grant Program under section 133 of title 23, United States Code;
- (B) the Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality Improvement Program under section 149 of such title;
- (C) the Capital Investment Grants program under chapter 53 of title 49, United States Code;
- (D) urbanized area formula grants under section 5307 of title 49, United States Code; and
- (E) such other transportation programs as the Secretary may designate by regulation.

### (4) ELIGIBLE JURISDICTION.—
The term "eligible jurisdiction" means any unit of general local government that—
- (A) receives covered funding directly or through a State; and
- (B) has land use authority over areas within one-half mile of a fixed transit station.

### (5) FIXED TRANSIT STATION.—
The term "fixed transit station" means—
- (A) a heavy rail station;
- (B) a light rail station;
- (C) a commuter rail station; or
- (D) a bus rapid transit station with dedicated right-of-way.

### (6) SECRETARY.—
The term "Secretary" means the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development.

### (7) TRANSIT-ORIENTED DEVELOPMENT ZONE.—
The term "transit-oriented development zone" means the area within one-half mile, measured as the crow flies, of any fixed transit station.

---

## SECTION 4. HOUSING OPPORTUNITY STANDARDS.

### (a) ESTABLISHMENT.—
Not later than 18 months after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary, in consultation with the Secretary of Transportation, shall establish Housing Opportunity Standards that eligible jurisdictions must meet to maintain full eligibility for covered funding.

### (b) MINIMUM STANDARDS.—
The Housing Opportunity Standards shall include, at a minimum, the following requirements:

#### (1) TRANSIT-ORIENTED DENSITY.—
Within transit-oriented development zones, eligible jurisdictions shall—
- (A) permit multifamily residential development by right at a minimum density of—
  - (i) 50 dwelling units per acre within one-quarter mile of heavy rail stations;
  - (ii) 40 dwelling units per acre within one-half mile of heavy rail stations and within one-quarter mile of light rail or commuter rail stations; and
  - (iii) 30 dwelling units per acre within one-half mile of light rail, commuter rail, or bus rapid transit stations;
- (B) not impose height limits of less than 4 stories within one-quarter mile of fixed transit stations; and
- (C) not require conditional use permits, planned unit development approval, or other discretionary review for projects meeting objective standards.

#### (2) ACCESSORY DWELLING UNITS.—
Eligible jurisdictions shall—
- (A) permit accessory dwelling units by right on any lot zoned for single-family residential use;
- (B) not require owner occupancy of either the primary dwelling or the accessory dwelling unit;
- (C) not require additional parking spaces for accessory dwelling units;
- (D) permit accessory dwelling units of at least 800 square feet or 50 percent of the primary dwelling floor area, whichever is greater; and
- (E) not impose design standards that would render accessory dwelling unit construction economically infeasible.

#### (3) PARKING REFORM.—
Within transit-oriented development zones, eligible jurisdictions shall—
- (A) not impose minimum parking requirements for residential development; and
- (B) permit shared parking arrangements among uses.

#### (4) APPROVAL TIMELINES.—
For residential development proposals within transit-oriented development zones that meet objective zoning and design standards, eligible jurisdictions shall—
- (A) determine application completeness within 30 days of submission;
- (B) complete all required reviews within 60 days of a complete application; and
- (C) issue building permits within 90 days of a complete application.

#### (5) DEEMED APPROVED.—
If an eligible jurisdiction fails to act on a complete application within the timeline specified in paragraph (4), the application shall be deemed approved.

### (c) ANTI-DISPLACEMENT PROTECTIONS.—
To be certified as meeting Housing Opportunity Standards, an eligible jurisdiction shall adopt and enforce the following protections:

#### (1) JUST-CAUSE EVICTION.—
Landlords may terminate a tenancy only for cause, including—
- (A) failure to pay rent;
- (B) material breach of lease terms;
- (C) nuisance or illegal activity;
- (D) owner or family member move-in (for owner-occupied buildings of 4 units or fewer); or
- (E) demolition or substantial rehabilitation (with relocation assistance under paragraph (4)).

#### (2) TENANT RIGHT OF FIRST REFUSAL.—
- (A) Before selling a property with 5 or more rental units, the owner shall provide written notice to all tenants and any qualified tenant organization.
- (B) Qualified tenant organizations shall have 90 days to match a bona fide purchase offer.

#### (3) INCLUSIONARY REQUIREMENTS.—
For residential developments of 10 or more units—
- (A) not less than 10 percent of units in developments of 10 to 49 units shall be affordable to households earning not more than 60 percent of area median income;
- (B) not less than 12 percent of units in developments of 50 to 99 units shall be so affordable; and
- (C) not less than 15 percent of units in developments of 100 or more units shall be affordable, with a mix of units affordable to households earning 30 percent, 50 percent, and 80 percent of area median income.

#### (4) RELOCATION ASSISTANCE.—
When tenants are displaced due to demolition or substantial rehabilitation—
- (A) landlords shall provide not less than 90 days' written notice;
- (B) tenants shall receive a relocation payment equal to 3 months' rent; and
- (C) tenants shall have a right of first refusal to occupy a comparable unit in the new development at a rent not exceeding the tenant's prior rent, adjusted for inflation.

## SECTION 5. CERTIFICATION AND COMPLIANCE.

### (a) CERTIFICATION PROCESS.—

#### (1) INITIAL CERTIFICATION.—
Not later than 4 years after the date of enactment of this Act, each eligible jurisdiction shall submit to the Secretary a certification that the jurisdiction meets Housing Opportunity Standards.

#### (2) ANNUAL RECERTIFICATION.—
Each certified jurisdiction shall submit annual recertifications demonstrating continued compliance.

#### (3) SELF-CERTIFICATION WITH AUDIT.—
- (A) Jurisdictions may self-certify compliance.
- (B) The Secretary shall conduct random audits of not less than 20 percent of certifications annually.

### (b) TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE.—

#### (1) AUTHORIZATION.—
There is authorized to be appropriated $100,000,000 for each fiscal year to provide technical assistance to eligible jurisdictions in meeting Housing Opportunity Standards.

#### (2) USE OF FUNDS.—
Technical assistance funds may be used for—
- (A) development of model ordinances;
- (B) planning capacity grants to small jurisdictions;
- (C) training for local planning officials; and
- (D) compliance monitoring systems.

### (c) EARLY ADOPTER PROGRAM.—

#### (1) INCENTIVE.—
Eligible jurisdictions that achieve certification within 3 years of enactment shall receive a 5 percent increase in their allocation of covered funding for 3 fiscal years.

#### (2) PILOT PROGRAM.—
The Secretary shall establish a pilot program for early adopter jurisdictions to test implementation approaches and share best practices.

---

## SECTION 6. CONSEQUENCES OF NON-COMPLIANCE.

### (a) GRADUATED SANCTIONS.—

#### (1) YEAR 4.—
Jurisdictions that fail to submit a compliance plan by year 4 shall receive—
- (A) written warning; and
- (B) priority access to technical assistance.

#### (2) YEAR 5.—
Jurisdictions that fail to demonstrate substantial progress toward compliance by year 5 shall have their allocation of covered funding reduced by 5 percent.

#### (3) YEAR 6.—
Jurisdictions that fail to achieve certification by year 6 shall be ineligible for discretionary grants under covered funding programs.

#### (4) YEAR 7 AND THEREAFTER.—
Jurisdictions that remain non-compliant in year 7 and thereafter shall have their allocation of covered funding reduced by up to 15 percent.

### (b) APPEALS.—

#### (1) RIGHT TO APPEAL.—
Any jurisdiction subject to sanctions may appeal to the Secretary.

#### (2) HEARING.—
The Secretary shall provide an opportunity for a hearing within 60 days of an appeal.

#### (3) FINAL DECISION.—
The Secretary shall issue a final decision within 30 days of the hearing.

### (c) RESTORATION.—
Sanctions shall be lifted upon certification of compliance.

---

## SECTION 7. PRESERVATION OF STATE AND LOCAL AUTHORITY.

### (a) ADDITIONAL REQUIREMENTS PERMITTED.—
Nothing in this Act shall be construed to prohibit a State or eligible jurisdiction from adopting requirements that exceed the Housing Opportunity Standards.

### (b) HISTORIC DISTRICTS.—
The Secretary may, by regulation, provide limited exemptions for designated historic districts, provided that—
- (1) the historic district was designated prior to the date of enactment of this Act; and
- (2) alternative density allowances are provided in locations outside the historic district.

### (c) ENVIRONMENTAL REVIEW.—
Nothing in this Act shall exempt any development from applicable Federal environmental review requirements under the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969.

---

## SECTION 8. DATA COLLECTION AND REPORTING.

### (a) DATA COLLECTION.—
The Secretary shall collect data on—
- (1) housing permits issued in transit-oriented development zones;
- (2) housing production trends in certified jurisdictions;
- (3) changes in housing costs in reformed areas; and
- (4) displacement and relocation patterns.

### (b) ANNUAL REPORT.—
Not later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, and annually thereafter, the Secretary shall submit to Congress a report on—
- (1) progress toward certification by eligible jurisdictions;
- (2) housing production and affordability trends; and
- (3) recommendations for program improvements.

---

## SECTION 9. REGULATIONS.

The Secretary, in consultation with the Secretary of Transportation, shall promulgate regulations to carry out this Act not later than 18 months after the date of enactment.

---

## SECTION 10. AUTHORIZATION OF APPROPRIATIONS.

There are authorized to be appropriated—

(1) $25,000,000 for fiscal year 2027 for rulemaking and initial implementation;

(2) $100,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2036 for technical assistance under section 5(b); and

(3) such sums as may be necessary for administration and enforcement.

---

## SECTION 11. EFFECTIVE DATE.

This Act shall take effect on October 1 following the date of enactment.

---

## SECTION 12. SEVERABILITY.

If any provision of this Act, or the application of such provision to any person or circumstance, is held to be unconstitutional, the remainder of this Act, and the application of the remaining provisions to any person or circumstance, shall not be affected.
