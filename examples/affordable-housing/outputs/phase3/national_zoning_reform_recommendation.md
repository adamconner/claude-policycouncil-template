# Policy Recommendation: National Zoning Reform Act

**Policy Council Phase 3 | Consolidated Recommendation**

---

## Executive Summary

This recommendation proposes conditioning federal infrastructure and transit funding on local zoning reforms that enable housing production near transit and employment centers. The policy leverages $80-100 billion in annual federal transportation funding to incentivize state and local zoning changes, without requiring new appropriations.

**Key Features:**
- Transit-oriented density requirements
- By-right approval pathways
- Parking reform near transit
- Strong anti-displacement protections
- 5-7 year implementation timeline

**Assessment:** The policy has **moderate political viability** with unusual bipartisan potential, **high expected benefits** relative to minimal federal costs, and addresses a well-documented market failure with strong research consensus.

## 1. Problem Statement

### The Housing Crisis by the Numbers

| Indicator | Current Status |
|-----------|----------------|
| National housing deficit | 3.8 million units (Bipartisan Policy Center, 2025) |
| Rent burden (>30% of income) | 50% of renters |
| Homeownership rate | 65.7% (down from 69% in 2004) |
| Median rent | $1,850/month (2024) |

### Root Cause: Restrictive Zoning

Academic research has conclusively established that local zoning restrictions are the primary driver of housing cost variation across U.S. cities:

- **Glaeser & Gyourko (2003, updated 2025):** Zoning regulations add 20-50% to housing costs in high-demand metros
- **Hsieh & Moretti (2019):** Housing constraints in productive cities reduced U.S. GDP growth by 36% from 1964-2009
- **Schuetz (2022):** Zoning reform is a necessary (though not sufficient) condition for affordability

### Why Local Action Has Failed

Local governments face collective action problems:
1. **Incumbents benefit from scarcity** — existing homeowners gain from high prices
2. **Costs are concentrated, benefits diffuse** — NIMBYs mobilize, future residents cannot
3. **Fiscal incentives misaligned** — single-family homes often generate more property tax per service demand
4. **Race to the bottom** — each jurisdiction can exclude, shifting housing burden elsewhere

Federal intervention is justified because housing constraints impose costs beyond local boundaries through reduced economic productivity, increased federal housing assistance, and constrained labor mobility.

## 2. Proposed Policy

### Mechanism

Condition receipt of federal transportation and infrastructure funding on state/local compliance with minimum zoning standards that enable housing production.

### Core Requirements

| Requirement | Standard | Rationale |
|-------------|----------|-----------|
| **Transit-oriented density** | Multifamily by-right within 0.5 miles of fixed transit | Maximize transit investment ROI |
| **ADU legalization** | Permitted in all residential zones | Gentle density, homeowner wealth |
| **Parking reform** | No minimums within 0.5 miles of transit | Reduce construction costs 15-20% |
| **Approval timelines** | 90-day by-right for compliant projects | Reduce carrying costs and uncertainty |

### Anti-Displacement Protections

| Protection | Implementation |
|------------|----------------|
| **Just-cause eviction** | Required for participating jurisdictions |
| **Tenant right of first refusal** | In redevelopment of occupied buildings |
| **Inclusionary requirements** | 10-15% affordable units (or in-lieu fee) |
| **Relocation assistance** | Minimum standards for displaced tenants |

### Funding Conditions

| Year | Requirement | Non-Compliance Consequence |
|------|-------------|---------------------------|
| 1-3 | Voluntary pilot program | 5% bonus for participants |
| 4 | Submit compliance plan | Warning; technical assistance |
| 5 | Demonstrate progress | 5% reduction in formula funds |
| 6 | Full compliance | Ineligibility for discretionary grants |
| 7+ | Sustained compliance | Up to 15% reduction |

### Distributional Effects

- Renters (44 million households)

- Existing homeowners (slower appreciation, 2-5% reduction in appreciation rates)
- Some incumbent landowners

## 7. Evidence Base

### High Confidence Findings

- Zoning restrictions increase housing costs in constrained markets
- By-right approval processes accelerate development
- Supply constraints reduce economic productivity and mobility
- Federal funding conditions can induce state/local policy changes

### Supporting International Evidence

| Jurisdiction | Reform | Result |
|--------------|--------|--------|
| Tokyo, Japan | National zoning system | 130,000 units/year (vs. NYC's 30,000) |
| Auckland, NZ | 2016 Unitary Plan | Rents 26-33% lower than counterfactual |
| Germany | Municipal incentive alignment | 60% rental market, historically stable prices |

### U.S. State Precedents

| State | Reform | Status |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Oregon | HB 2001 (end single-family zoning) | Compliance achieved by 2024 |
| California | ADU reforms | 65,000 units added statewide |
| Minneapolis | 2040 Plan | 16-34% rent reduction vs. counterfactual |

## 8. Recommendations

### Primary Recommendation

Pursue the National Zoning Reform Act as a **component of the next infrastructure reauthorization bill**, targeting early 2026 legislative window.

### Key Design Principles

1. **Clear, objective standards** — jurisdictions must know exactly what compliance requires
2. **Adequate phase-in** — 5-7 years allows code revision without rushed implementation
3. **Robust technical assistance** — capacity-limited jurisdictions need support
4. **Strong anti-displacement** — protections must be genuine, not token
5. **Consistent enforcement** — credibility requires follow-through

### Coalition-Building Amendments

| Amendment | Target Constituency | Recommended |
|-----------|--------------------| ------------|
| Extended phase-in (7 years) | Local governments | Yes |
| Technical assistance grants | Small cities | Yes |
| Prevailing wage requirements | Building trades | Consider |
| Anti-displacement enhancements | Civil rights groups | Yes |
| Grandfather existing homeowners | Suburban Republicans | Negotiate |

### Amendments to Resist

- Full exemptions for any jurisdiction category
- Implementation beyond 7 years
- Removal of transit-density linkage
- Weakening of anti-displacement provisions

## 9. Conclusion

The National Zoning Reform Act represents a rare policy opportunity: addressing a well-documented market failure with strong research consensus, unusual bipartisan potential, minimal federal costs, and significant benefits for working families.

The policy does not guarantee affordable housing — no single policy can. But it removes a major barrier to housing production that has constrained economic growth, limited mobility, and increased inequality for decades.

**Bottom line:** This policy is viable, evidence-based, and consequential. It deserves priority attention in the next Congress.
