# Economic Analysis: National Zoning Reform

**Policy Council Phase 3 | Economist Analysis**

---

## Executive Summary

Conditioning federal infrastructure funding on zoning reform represents a fiscally efficient approach that leverages ~$80-100 billion in annual federal transportation funding without new appropriations. Analysis projects housing cost reductions of 8-15% in reformed areas over a decade, with substantial GDP gains from improved labor mobility.

---

## 1. Cost Estimation

### Federal Government Costs

| Category | Annual Cost | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Administrative implementation | $15-25M | Compliance monitoring, TA coordination |
| New appropriations required | $0 | Restructures existing funding streams |
| Technical assistance | $100M | Support for local compliance |
| **Total Federal Cost** | **$115-125M/year** | Minimal relative to housing benefits |

### State/Local Compliance Costs

| Item | Range per Jurisdiction |
|------|------------------------|
| Zoning code revisions | $50,000 - $500,000 |
| Environmental review updates | $25,000 - $150,000 |
| Staff training | $10,000 - $75,000 |
| Public engagement | $20,000 - $100,000 |
| **Total** | **$200K - $1.8M one-time** |

Compliance costs are minimal relative to federal funding at stake (typical mid-sized cities receive $10-50M annually in transportation funds).

---

## 2. Benefit Quantification

### Housing Supply and Price Effects

| Scenario | State Adoption | Supply Increase | Price Reduction |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Conservative | 30% | 3-5% nationally | 5-8% in reformed areas |
| Moderate | 60% | 8-12% nationally | 10-15% in reformed areas |
| Optimistic | 80% | 15-20% nationally | 15-22% in reformed areas |

### Quantified Household Savings

- Median U.S. rent: $1,850/month (2024)
- 10% reduction = **$2,220 annual savings** per renting household
- With 44 million renter households, 5% reduction across half of markets = **$20+ billion annual savings**

### GDP Impact

Based on Hsieh-Moretti framework (adjusted for partial implementation):
- Wage gains: 5-10% for workers who relocate to higher-productivity areas
- Aggregate GDP impact: **0.5-2% increase over 15 years**
- Dollar terms: **$130-520 billion cumulative GDP gains**

### Construction Employment

- Each 1 million housing units = 2.9 million job-years
- 300,000 additional units/year = 870,000 job-years
- With multiplier effects: 1.2-1.6 million jobs supported annually

## 3. Distributional Analysis

### Who Benefits

| Group | Benefit |
|-------|---------|
| **Renters** (44M households) | Direct cost reduction |
| **First-time homebuyers** | Expanded inventory, lower prices |
| **Workers in high-cost metros** | Access to productive labor markets |
| **Low/moderate income** | Housing burden falls disproportionately on lower quintiles |

### Who May Experience Costs

| Group | Impact | Magnitude |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Existing homeowners | Slower appreciation | 2-5% reduction in appreciation rates |
| Incumbent landowners | Changed expectations | Modest |
| Some small businesses | Commercial rent increases | Mitigated by expanded customer base |

## 4. Fiscal Impact Summary (10-Year)

| Level | Impact | Mechanism |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Federal | +$50-150B | Income tax on higher wages, reduced housing assistance |
| State | +$30-80B | Sales tax on construction, income tax |
| Local | +$20-60B | Property tax on new development |

**Net fiscal impact: Positive at all levels of government**

---

## 5. Sensitivity Analysis

### Scenario: Low Uptake (20-30% adoption)
- Supply increase limited to 2-4%
- Price effects concentrated in early-adopter areas
- GDP gains reduced to 0.2-0.5%

### Scenario: Strong Anti-Displacement Provisions
If provisions add 15-25% to development costs:
- Production reduced 20-35% vs. reform without provisions
- Net price effects smaller (5-10% vs. 10-15%)
- Trade-off: Production vs. affordability targeting

### Scenario: High Interest Rates (7%+)
- Near-term production response dampened
- Structural reforms preserve potential for future response
- Policy creates option value

---

## Key Economic Findings

1. **High benefit-cost ratio:** Minimal federal cost ($115-125M/year) generates $20-50B in annual housing savings
2. **Progressive distribution:** Benefits accrue to renters, first-time buyers, workers
3. **Fiscally positive:** Net revenue gains at all government levels
4. **Robust under uncertainty:** Even conservative scenarios yield meaningful benefits
