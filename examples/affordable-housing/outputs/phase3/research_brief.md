## 1. Academic Literature Review

### Foundational Research

**Glaeser & Gyourko (2003, updated 2018, 2025)**
The definitive work establishing zoning as the dominant factor in housing cost variation across U.S. cities. Their "zoning tax" methodology calculates the implicit cost of land-use restrictions by comparing home prices to construction costs. Key finding: In high-cost metros, regulations add 20-50% to housing costs. Their 2025 Brookings update shows construction costs increased 35% since 2000, primarily due to regulatory burden.

**Hsieh & Moretti (2019) - "Housing Constraints and Spatial Misallocation"**
Landmark study quantifying macroeconomic costs of housing constraints. Key finding: Relaxing housing constraints in high-productivity cities (New York, San Francisco, San Jose) could have increased aggregate U.S. GDP growth by **36%** from 1964-2009. The mechanism: workers cannot relocate to higher-productivity areas due to housing costs.

**Schuetz (2022) - "Fixer Upper: How to Repair America's Broken Housing Systems"**
Comprehensive policy analysis identifying zoning reform as necessary but insufficient condition for housing affordability. Recommends combining supply-side reforms with demand-side affordability programs.

## 2. International Examples

### Japan: National Zoning System
Japan's 12-zone national system (vs. thousands of local codes in US) enables dramatically higher housing production:
- **Tokyo:** 130,000 units/year vs. NYC's 30,000
- **Housing satisfaction:** 70% (vs. 65% in US)
- **Homelessness rate:** 0.2 per 10,000 (vs. 18 in US)

Key lesson: National standards create predictability for developers and prevent local veto points.

### Germany: Municipal Incentive Alignment
German system aligns municipal fiscal incentives with housing production through revenue-sharing mechanisms. Result: 60% rental market, historically stable prices (though recent pressure from immigration).

### Auckland, New Zealand: Most Ambitious Reform
2016 Unitary Plan represents most comprehensive single reform globally:
- 300% increase in developable capacity
- Construction doubled within 3 years
- Rents 26-33% lower than counterfactual by 2023

Key lesson: Comprehensive reform yields measurable results within 5 years.

## 3. U.S. State-Level Examples

### Oregon HB 2001 (2019)
First state to end single-family-only zoning statewide.

| Outcome | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Compliance | Achieved by 2024 deadline |
| Production | Slower than hoped; other barriers remain |
| Legal challenges | Survived initial challenges |
| 2024-2025 enhancements | State override authority added |

### California: Mixed Results
- **ADUs:** Success story — 40% of LA permits now ADUs
- **SB 9 (duplex):** Underperforming due to complexity
- **Builder's Remedy:** Effective enforcement mechanism

### Minneapolis 2040
- Promising rent/price moderation (16-34% lower than counterfactual)
- Currently under legal challenge
- Results suggest reform works when implemented

## 6. Evidence Assessment

### High Confidence
- Zoning restrictions increase housing costs in constrained markets
- By-right approval processes accelerate development
- Supply constraints reduce economic productivity and mobility

### Moderate Confidence
- Upzoning moderates rents/prices over 5-10 year horizon
- State preemption can overcome local resistance
- Anti-displacement provisions can be designed effectively

### Uncertain / Needs More Research
- Optimal anti-displacement policy bundle
- Long-term political sustainability of reforms
- Displacement magnitude absent protections
- Interaction effects with interest rates and construction costs

---

## Policy Design Implications

The proposed policy's anti-displacement protections (just-cause eviction, rent stabilization, inclusionary zoning) align with research recommendations, with caveats:
- **Rent stabilization:** Mixed long-term evidence; should be paired with production incentives
- **Inclusionary zoning:** 10-15% requirements do not significantly deter development
- **Just-cause eviction:** Strong evidence of tenant stability benefits
