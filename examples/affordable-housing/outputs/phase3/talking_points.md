# Talking Points: National Zoning Reform

**Policy Council Phase 3 | Advocate Messaging Guide**

---

## Universal Message (All Audiences)

> "Local zoning laws are blocking Americans from affordable housing near jobs and opportunity. This policy removes government barriers to building homes — without new federal spending."

---

## For Conservative Audiences

### Lead Message
**"Removing government barriers to housing construction"**

### Key Points

1. **This is deregulation, not new regulation**
   - Local zoning IS government regulation
   - Single-family mandates restrict what property owners can build
   - This restores property rights, not limits them

2. **Zero new federal spending**
   - Restructures existing infrastructure funding
   - No new appropriations required
   - Market solutions, not government programs

3. **Economists across the spectrum agree**
   - Cato Institute supports zoning reform
   - AEI researchers have documented regulatory costs
   - Rare consensus from left and right

4. **Proven precedent**
   - Highway funding tied to drinking age (1984)
   - Highway funding tied to speed limits
   - States remain free to decline federal funds

### Talking Point
> "If conservatives believe in free markets, we should oppose government regulations that prevent landowners from building housing. Local zoning boards shouldn't dictate what you can build on your own property."

---

## For Progressive Audiences

### Lead Message
**"Housing justice with community protections"**

### Key Points

1. **Exclusionary zoning has racist origins**
   - Single-family zoning designed to segregate
   - Continues to exclude low-income families
   - Reform is a civil rights issue

2. **Strong anti-displacement protections built in**
   - Just-cause eviction requirements
   - Tenant right of first refusal
   - Inclusionary zoning mandates
   - Relocation assistance

3. **Environmental benefits**
   - Density near transit reduces emissions
   - Sprawl is climate-harmful
   - Environmental groups support reform

4. **Addresses inequality**
   - Housing costs burden lowest-income families most
   - Workers deserve to live near jobs
   - Economic mobility requires housing access

### Talking Point
> "For too long, wealthy neighborhoods have used zoning to keep out working families. This policy opens up opportunity while protecting existing residents from displacement."

---

## For Business Audiences

### Lead Message
**"Workers need housing near jobs"**

### Key Points

1. **Housing costs hurt business competitiveness**
   - Can't recruit talent to unaffordable cities
   - Employees commute hours, reducing productivity
   - Housing shortage is an economic crisis

2. **Supply is the solution**
   - More housing units = lower prices
   - Construction creates jobs
   - Real estate industry supports reform

3. **GDP impact is massive**
   - $130-520 billion cumulative economic gains
   - 1.2-1.6 million jobs supported
   - Unlocks worker mobility to high-productivity areas

### Talking Point
> "When a teacher can't afford to live in the city where she teaches, something is broken. Business needs housing supply to compete for talent."

---

## For Local Government Officials

### Lead Message
**"Technical support and flexible timelines"**

### Key Points

1. **Generous phase-in period (5-7 years)**
   - Time to update zoning codes
   - Technical assistance provided
   - Early adopters get bonus funding

2. **Clear, objective standards**
   - Know exactly what compliance looks like
   - Model ordinances provided
   - No ambiguity or arbitrary enforcement

3. **Funding at stake is significant**
   - Typical mid-size city: $10-50M annually
   - Non-compliance affects discretionary grants first
   - Formula funds affected only after years of non-compliance

### Talking Point
> "This isn't a federal takeover — it's partnership. We'll provide the tools, resources, and time you need to update your codes."

---

## Responses to Common Objections

### "This violates local control"

> "Local governments remain free to decline federal funds. But if they accept federal transit dollars, it makes sense to allow housing near that transit. That's not a federal mandate — it's a funding condition, just like highway safety standards."

### "This will change neighborhood character"

> "Neighborhoods change over time regardless. The question is whether we plan for housing near transit or force sprawl development on the edges of cities. Smart growth preserves more land and creates walkable communities."

### "This will cause displacement"

> "That's why strong anti-displacement protections are built in: just-cause eviction, tenant right of first refusal, inclusionary requirements, and relocation assistance. More housing supply actually reduces displacement pressure by moderating prices."

### "Construction won't happen anyway due to costs"

> "Removing regulatory barriers is necessary but not sufficient. However, even when construction is slow, the reforms create option value — when conditions improve, housing can be built. And evidence from Auckland shows comprehensive reform does increase production."
