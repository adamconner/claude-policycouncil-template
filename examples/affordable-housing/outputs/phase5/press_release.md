### Bipartisan Coalition Unveils National Zoning Reform Act to Address Housing Crisis

*Proposal conditions infrastructure funding on local zoning reforms, requires no new spending*

---

**WASHINGTON, D.C.** — A bipartisan coalition of lawmakers today introduced the National Zoning Reform Act of 2026, landmark legislation designed to address America's housing shortage by conditioning federal infrastructure funding on local adoption of housing-friendly zoning policies.

The legislation responds to a growing consensus among economists across the political spectrum that restrictive local zoning is the primary driver of housing unaffordability in high-cost metropolitan areas.

"Workers deserve to be able to afford to live near their jobs," said [Sponsor Name]. "This bill removes government barriers to housing construction without spending a single new federal dollar. It's common-sense reform that economists on the left and right have called for."

### Key Provisions

The National Zoning Reform Act would require state and local governments receiving federal transportation funding to:

- **Allow housing near transit:** Multifamily development permitted by-right within half a mile of transit stations
- **Legalize accessory dwelling units:** "Granny flats" and backyard cottages allowed in residential neighborhoods
- **Reform parking requirements:** No parking minimums near transit
- **Streamline approvals:** 90-day maximum for qualifying projects

### Strong Anti-Displacement Protections

Unlike previous reform proposals, the bill includes robust protections for existing residents:

- Just-cause eviction requirements
- Tenant right of first refusal when properties are sold
- Inclusionary zoning mandates (10-15% affordable units)
- Relocation assistance for displaced tenants

"We can expand housing supply while protecting the communities that already exist," said [Co-Sponsor Name]. "These protections ensure that the benefits of reform reach everyone, not just new residents."

### Economic Impact

According to supporting research:

- America faces a shortage of nearly 4 million housing units
- Zoning restrictions add 20-50% to housing costs in constrained markets
- The policy could save renters $20+ billion annually
- GDP gains of $130-520 billion are projected over 15 years

### Bipartisan Support

The bill has attracted support across ideological lines:

**Conservative supporters** cite the bill's focus on removing government regulation and restoring property rights without new spending.

**Progressive supporters** highlight the equity dimensions of zoning reform and the bill's strong tenant protections.

"This is one of those rare issues where the evidence is so clear that people who agree on almost nothing else can agree on this," said [Expert Name], economist at [Institution]. "Local zoning is government regulation that restricts what property owners can build. Reforming it should appeal to anyone who believes in functioning markets or housing as a human right."

### Coalition Backing

The legislation is supported by a broad coalition including:

- YIMBY Alliance
- National Association of Realtors
- [Environmental Organization]
- [Civil Rights Organization]
- [Business Coalition]

### Timeline

The bill would:

- Allow 3 years for voluntary "early adopter" participation with funding bonuses
- Require compliance plans by year 4
- Phase in funding conditions over years 5-7
- Provide $100 million annually in technical assistance

### Background

Federal funding conditions have been used successfully to achieve national policy goals, including the national minimum drinking age and highway safety standards. The Supreme Court has upheld such conditions when they are clearly related to federal spending interests.
