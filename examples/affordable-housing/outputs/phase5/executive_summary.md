# National Zoning Reform Act
## Executive Summary

**Policy Council Phase 5 | 5-Page Overview**

---

## The Challenge

America faces its worst housing shortage since World War II. Nearly 4 million units are needed just to meet current demand. In high-cost metros, restrictive local zoning adds 20-50% to housing costs, blocking workers from economic opportunity and driving inequality.

**The core problem:** Local governments have every incentive to restrict housing. Existing homeowners benefit from scarcity. Future residents can't vote in current zoning decisions. The costs of exclusion are pushed onto the region while benefits stay local.

The result: a collective action failure that individual cities cannot solve.

---

## The Solution

**Condition federal infrastructure and transit funding on local zoning reforms that enable housing production.**

This approach:
- Leverages $80-100 billion in annual federal transportation funding
- Requires no new federal appropriations
- Uses proven funding-condition precedents (highway speed limits, drinking age)
- Includes strong protections against displacement

### Core Requirements

| Reform | Standard |
|--------|----------|
| **Transit-oriented density** | Multifamily by-right within 0.5 miles of transit |
| **Accessory dwelling units** | Permitted in all residential zones |
| **Parking reform** | No minimums near transit |
| **Approval timelines** | 90-day by-right approvals |

### Built-In Protections

| Protection | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| Just-cause eviction | Prevent displacement of existing tenants |
| Tenant right of first refusal | Community stability |
| Inclusionary requirements | 10-15% affordable units in new development |
| Relocation assistance | Support for displaced residents |

---

## Why This Works

### 1. Addresses Root Cause

Research conclusively shows that zoning restrictions — not construction costs or interest rates — are the primary driver of housing cost variation across cities. This policy targets the root cause.

### 2. Clear Legal Precedent

Federal funding conditions have been upheld repeatedly:
- National drinking age (1984): Highway funds conditioned on age 21
- Highway speed limits (1974): Highway funds conditioned on 55 mph
- Civil rights (1964): All federal funds conditioned on non-discrimination

The Supreme Court's *South Dakota v. Dole* framework supports this approach.

### 3. Bipartisan Economic Consensus

Economists across the political spectrum agree that zoning restrictions harm housing affordability:
- **Conservative:** Cato Institute, AEI researchers call it government overregulation
- **Progressive:** Brookings, academic economists focus on equity impacts
- **Consensus:** Rare left-right agreement that zoning reform is needed

### 4. No New Spending

Unlike housing vouchers or public housing, this policy restructures existing funding streams. The federal cost is approximately $120 million annually — administrative expenses only.

## Addressing Concerns

### "This violates local control"

Local governments remain free to decline federal funds. But if they accept federal transit dollars, requiring housing near that transit is a reasonable condition — just like highway safety standards.

### "This will cause displacement"

That's why strong anti-displacement protections are built into the policy: just-cause eviction, tenant right of first refusal, inclusionary requirements, and relocation assistance. More housing supply actually reduces displacement pressure by moderating prices.

### "Neighborhoods will change"

Neighborhoods change regardless. The question is whether we plan for housing near transit or force sprawl development at the edges. Smart growth preserves more land and creates walkable communities.

### "Construction won't happen anyway"

Removing regulatory barriers is necessary even if not sufficient. When interest rates fall or construction costs moderate, reformed areas will be ready to build. The policy creates option value.

## The Bottom Line

The National Zoning Reform Act represents a rare policy opportunity:

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Problem | Well-documented, research consensus |
| Solution | Addresses root cause directly |
| Precedent | Proven constitutional approach |
| Cost | Minimal federal expense |
| Benefits | Substantial and broadly distributed |
| Politics | Unusual bipartisan potential |

**This policy is viable, evidence-based, and consequential.**

It won't solve the housing crisis alone — no single policy can. But it removes a major barrier that has constrained economic growth, limited mobility, and increased inequality for decades.

The question is not whether zoning reform is needed. The question is whether we're willing to act.

---

## Recommended Action

**Include the National Zoning Reform Act in the 2026 infrastructure reauthorization bill.**

This vehicle provides:
- Must-pass legislation with bipartisan tradition
- Clear policy nexus (housing-transit connection)
- Existing funding streams to condition
- Proven precedent for funding conditions
