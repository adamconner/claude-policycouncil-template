# National Zoning Reform Act
## Fact Sheet

**Policy Council Phase 5 | Quick Reference**

---

## What Is This Bill?

The National Zoning Reform Act conditions federal infrastructure and transit funding on state and local adoption of housing-friendly zoning policies.

**Bottom line:** If you want federal transit dollars, allow housing near transit.

---

## The Problem

| Fact | Number |
|------|--------|
| National housing shortage | **3.8 million units** |
| Renters spending 30%+ on housing | **50%** |
| Cost increase from zoning restrictions | **20-50%** |
| GDP lost to housing constraints | **36%** (1964-2009) |

---

## The Solution

### What Localities Must Do

| Requirement | Standard |
|-------------|----------|
| **Transit-area housing** | Multifamily by-right within 0.5 miles of transit |
| **Accessory dwelling units** | Allowed in all residential zones |
| **Parking minimums** | Eliminated near transit |
| **Approval timelines** | 90 days maximum for qualifying projects |

### Protections for Existing Residents

| Protection | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Just-cause eviction** | Can't evict without cause |
| **First refusal rights** | Tenants can match sale offers |
| **Affordability mandates** | 10-15% affordable units required |
| **Relocation assistance** | 3 months rent + moving costs |

## Constitutional Basis

*South Dakota v. Dole* (1987) established funding conditions are constitutional if:

- ✓ Related to federal spending power
- ✓ Conditions are unambiguous
- ✓ Related to federal interest
- ✓ Not coercive (<10% of budget)

This bill meets all tests.

---

## What This Bill Does NOT Do

- ❌ No federal mandate on localities
- ❌ No takeover of local planning
- ❌ No new federal spending
- ❌ No one-size-fits-all design standards
- ❌ No requirement to accept federal funds

States and localities remain free to decline federal funding.
