# Refined Talking Points
## National Zoning Reform Act

**Policy Council Phase 5 | Final Messaging Guide**

---

## Core Message (30 Seconds)

> "Local zoning laws are blocking Americans from affordable housing near jobs and opportunity. The National Zoning Reform Act removes these government barriers — without new federal spending — while protecting existing residents from displacement. Economists across the political spectrum agree this is needed."

---

## The Elevator Pitch (60 Seconds)

> "America is nearly 4 million homes short of what we need. Research shows that local zoning restrictions add 20-50% to housing costs in high-demand cities.
>
> The National Zoning Reform Act conditions federal transit funding on local adoption of commonsense reforms: allowing housing near transit, legalizing backyard cottages, and speeding up approvals.
>
> This isn't a federal takeover — localities can decline federal funds. It's the same approach we've used for highway safety and the drinking age. And it includes strong protections against displacement.
>
> No new spending. Bipartisan economic consensus. Real results."

---

## Audience-Specific Framing

### Conservative Audiences

**Theme:** Deregulation and Property Rights

**Key points:**
1. Local zoning IS government regulation
2. Single-family mandates restrict what owners can build
3. Zero new federal spending
4. Cato, AEI economists support reform
5. Uses proven funding-condition approach

**Lead message:**
> "If we believe in free markets, we should oppose government regulations that prevent landowners from building housing on their own property."

**Avoid:** Emphasizing federal role, anti-displacement provisions

---

### Progressive Audiences

**Theme:** Housing Justice and Equity

**Key points:**
1. Single-family zoning has racist origins
2. Exclusionary zoning perpetuates segregation
3. Strong anti-displacement protections built in
4. Benefits lower-income households most
5. Environmental benefits (density = less sprawl)

**Lead message:**
> "For too long, wealthy neighborhoods have used zoning to exclude working families. This policy opens up opportunity while protecting existing communities."

**Avoid:** Emphasizing market benefits, property rights framing

---

### Business Audiences

**Theme:** Workforce and Competitiveness

**Key points:**
1. Workers need housing near jobs
2. Housing costs hurt talent recruitment
3. Construction creates jobs
4. Massive GDP gains projected
5. NAR, tech industry support

**Lead message:**
> "When a teacher can't afford to live in the city where she teaches, employers suffer too. Housing supply is an economic competitiveness issue."

**Avoid:** Policy details, legal/regulatory language

---

### Local Government Officials

**Theme:** Partnership and Support

**Key points:**
1. Generous 5-7 year phase-in
2. $100M/year in technical assistance
3. Clear, objective standards
4. Early adopter bonuses
5. Model ordinances provided

**Lead message:**
> "This isn't a mandate — it's partnership. We'll provide the tools, resources, and time you need to update your codes. Early movers get funding bonuses."

**Avoid:** Emphasizing penalties, federal authority

## Handling Tough Questions

### "Doesn't this violate local control?"

> "Local governments remain free to decline federal funds. But if they accept federal transit dollars, it makes sense to require housing near that transit. That's not a mandate — it's a funding condition, just like highway safety standards. We've done this successfully for decades."

### "Won't this cause displacement?"

> "That's exactly why we included the strongest anti-displacement protections in any federal housing legislation: just-cause eviction, tenant right of first refusal, affordability requirements, and relocation assistance. More housing supply actually reduces displacement pressure by moderating prices."

### "What about neighborhood character?"

> "Neighborhoods change over time regardless of zoning. The question is whether we plan for housing near transit — creating walkable communities — or push development to the edges of cities as sprawl. The choice isn't change or no change. It's smart growth or sprawl."

### "Construction won't happen due to costs/rates"

> "Removing regulatory barriers is necessary even when it's not sufficient. When conditions improve — when rates fall, when supply chains normalize — reformed areas will be ready to build. Cities that cling to exclusionary zoning will miss the opportunity. The policy creates option value."

### "Why should the federal government be involved?"

> "Housing constraints don't stop at city lines. When San Francisco restricts housing, it affects the entire regional economy and pushes workers into long commutes. When productive cities exclude housing, it reduces national GDP. And federal transit investments are wasted without nearby housing. The federal interest is clear."

## Message Don'ts

| Avoid | Instead |
|-------|---------|
| "Mandate" | "Funding condition" |
| "Force cities" | "Incentivize reform" |
| "Federal takeover" | "Federal-local partnership" |
| "Abolish zoning" | "Reform outdated restrictions" |
| "Dense development" | "Housing near transit" |
| "End single-family neighborhoods" | "Allow more housing types" |

## Quick-Reference Card

**What it does:** Conditions transit funding on zoning reform

**What it requires:**
- Housing near transit
- ADUs allowed
- No parking minimums
- 90-day approvals

**What it protects:**
- Just-cause eviction
- Tenant first refusal
- 10-15% affordable
- Relocation assistance

**Key numbers:**
- $0 new spending
- $20B+ annual savings
- 600:1 benefit-cost
- 5-7 year phase-in
